# E11 — Grand Fair Comparison (Authoritative)

**Purpose.** The authoritative, strictly fair comparison across all 9 paper benchmarks × 6 methods × two parameter budgets (matched within ±10% of SV-SNN, and a "rich" budget of 50k–140k params), to isolate the structural advantage from the capacity advantage. This experiment is the source of the manuscript's main comparison and computational-cost tables.

**Reviewer comments addressed.** R3.1, R5.2, R7.3, R5.7, R9.5, R3.4, R5.3, R9.1, R8.6, R9.2.

## Setup

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Benchmarks | Heat 20π, Helmholtz 24π, nonlinear elliptic, Heat 500π, Helmholtz cylinder, Helmholtz 48π, Poisson porous, Taylor-Green, dual-cylinder NS |
| Methods | SV-SNN, SPINN, SIREN, FourierPINN, PINN, classical Fourier Galerkin (cases 2 & 6 only) |
| Budgets | matched ±10% of SV-SNN; "rich" = original large nets |
| Training | 10,000 epochs (cases 1–8), 15,000 (case 9); Adam lr = 1e-3; same `w_char` init |
| Scale | 245 runs, 83 groups, 3 seeds each, 0 failures |

## Conclusions

- SV-SNN is best on **9/9 cases** at the matched budget (1.9×–94× lead) and remains best at the rich budget (2.1×–99×).
- The advantage is attributed to the separable structure + spectral basis + analytic derivatives, not to parameter count.
- Speed and memory gains are strongest at high collocation density (C8 ~2× faster than SPINN; C9 ~4–5× faster with ~6× less GPU memory).
