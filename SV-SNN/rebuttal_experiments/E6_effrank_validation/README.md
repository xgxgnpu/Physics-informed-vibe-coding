# E6 — Effective Rank Validation

**Purpose.** Validate that the residual-Jacobian effective rank predicts high-frequency trainability: SV-SNN maintains a high effective rank while a vanilla PINN's rank collapses, mechanistically explaining spectral bias.

**Reviewer comments addressed.** R1.3, R3.3, R8.2.

## Setup

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Metric | `r_eff(0.99)` from the SVD of the residual Jacobian on 300 collocation points |
| PDE | 2D Helmholtz, κ ∈ {20π, 40π, 80π} |
| Methods | accelerated SV-SNN vs vanilla PINN (4×128 tanh) |
| Grid | `NC = 160` (≥4 pts/wavelength, avoids the E2 aliasing regime) |
| Seeds | 3 |

## Key results

| κ | Method | Early `r_eff` (≈ ep 500) | Final `r_eff` | Final L2 (mean) |
|---|--------|-------------------------:|--------------:|----------------:|
| 20π | **SV-SNN** | ~150 | ~150 | **0.026** |
| 20π | PINN | **3** | ~50 | 1.02 |
| 40π | **SV-SNN** | ~173 | ~189 | **0.028** |
| 40π | PINN | **3** | ~42 | 1.05 |
| 80π | **SV-SNN** | ~195 | ~219 | **0.079** |
| 80π | PINN | **3** | ~83 | 1.06 |

Predictivity: early `r_eff ≈ 3` → final L2 ≈ 1.0 (PINN failure); early `r_eff > 100` → final L2 < 0.1 (SV-SNN success).

## Conclusions

- The vanilla PINN Jacobian effective rank **collapses to ~3** early in training, matching its complete failure (L2 ≈ 1); SV-SNN stays at ~150–250 and converges (L2 0.026–0.079).
- The initial singular spectra are revealing: the PINN spectrum decays sharply while SV-SNN stays flat — a mechanistic explanation for spectral bias.
- Early effective rank is therefore a **predictive / prescriptive** metric, not merely a post-hoc diagnostic.
